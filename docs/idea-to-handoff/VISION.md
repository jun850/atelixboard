# Vision

**From a shared idea to an owned next step, without losing the context.**

The long-term ambition is a smooth office experience: whiteboarding, project files, and documents connected around the work people are trying to complete. The initial product earns that breadth through one repeatable workflow: **idea → handoff → detail**.

## Problem

A workshop produces sketches, screenshots, and decisions. The next person still has to ask: what did we choose, what should I do, which version matters, and where are the supporting files? Teams repeatedly reconstruct context between a whiteboard, a message, a folder, and a document.

Our hypothesis is that this transition creates expensive clarification, rework, and delays. It is not yet validated by customer interviews or measured usage. The first research must distinguish this pain from ordinary task tracking and infrequent workshop administration.

## Initial customer

Start with software consultancies and product/design agencies of roughly 10–100 employees that regularly hand work between discovery, design, and implementation. The buyer hypothesis is the founder, delivery lead, or head of design; the champion is the person running discovery. Initial recipients are internal project members. External client guest access follows only after permissions work across every surface.

This is a beachhead hypothesis, not a claim that these firms already use or will pay for Boardy. Product teams are an adjacent expansion segment. Do not target every office worker in the first release.

## What we sell

A recipient opens a small, understandable handoff: the relevant idea, why it matters, the owner, the next action, and what done means. They accept it or ask for a change. Only then does the team expand it into a specification, proposal, checklist, or other useful document. Related files remain attached to the same project and handoff.

The unit of value is an **accepted, useful handoff**. A board, file, or generated document is an input or output, not proof that the product delivered value.

## Why now, and why this could become a company

Collaborative editors and open-source components lower the cost of building a capable workspace. That also lowers competitors' costs. AI can help draft detail from approved context, but generating more text alone is not differentiation.

The venture thesis is that Boardy can become the durable record of how an idea became a decision, an owner, and an outcome. That could support recurring team budgets and expansion across projects. This thesis must be earned through retention and workflow adoption; assembling Excalidraw and Docmost does not establish a moat.

## Market model: assumptions to validate

Avoid adding whiteboarding, knowledge management, file storage, and task-management market estimates together; those budgets overlap. Start with annual spend per reachable customer.

| Scenario | Explicit assumptions | Annual revenue pool |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Illustrative broad TAM | 1 million qualifying organizations × 20 paid users × $15/month | $3.6 billion |
| Illustrative beachhead SAM | 50,000 reachable agencies/consultancies × 20 paid users × $15/month | $180 million |
| Illustrative five-year SOM | 1,000 customers × 20 paid users × $15/month | $3.6 million ARR |
| Venture-scale milestone | About 27,778 customers × 20 paid users × $15/month | About $100 million ARR |

These are **sensitivity scenarios, not researched organization counts or forecasts**. They expose the underwriting question: can the company reach enough customers, increase customer value, or expand beyond the first segment? A narrow agency tool can be a good business without meeting a venture return profile. Before fundraising, replace counts with a sourced, deduplicated customer universe and demonstrate attainable acquisition economics.

## Business model and go-to-market hypotheses

- Keep a low-friction canvas entry point. Charge teams for the durable handoff workflow, project organization, and governance once value is demonstrated.
- Test a team minimum plus paid active contributors. An initial research range is $12–20 per contributor/month, with a $99–199 team minimum; these are test prices, not approved billing changes. Existing marketing prices remain unchanged until deliberately revised.
- Put paid limits around team workflow scale, history, storage, and administration. Avoid making recipient acceptance a surprise paywall. Meter costly AI separately or bound its allowance.
- Start with 10 design partners and a single real project each. Observe actual handoffs rather than collecting feature wish lists.
- Acquire the next customers through hands-on founder demos and concrete workflow templates. Invitations to recipients can create distribution, but external invitations are not permission to expose private projects.
- Expand within an account from one project to repeated projects, then multiple teams. Consider broader workflow segments only after retention is visible.

Bootstrap the first workflow with the current editor, managed infrastructure, and manual document export. Do not make enterprise integrations, self-hosting support, or unlimited AI costs prerequisites to learning. Contribution margin must include hosting, storage, document operations and fork maintenance, model costs, and support.

## Competitive reality

Excalidraw, Miro, and FigJam set expectations for visual collaboration; Notion and Confluence for knowledge; Microsoft and Google for office distribution; ClickUp and similar tools for consolidated workflows. This is a positioning comparison, not a current feature or pricing audit. Docmost already supports Excalidraw diagrams. A combined editor is therefore a starting capability, not a unique proposition.

Win on how quickly a recipient understands and accepts responsibility, and how well the subsequent work retains context. The defensibility hypothesis is accumulated project relationships, reusable handoff patterns, trusted permissions, and habitual cross-team usage. Claims of proprietary data advantage require evidence and customers' rights to use that data.

## Founder and investment assessment

The existing Boardy implementation demonstrates ability to ship a product foundation. It does not establish founder-market fit, repeatable distribution, or willingness to pay. Founder credibility should be supported by observed customer access, domain experience, delivery speed, and a record of converting feedback into retention. Do not invent team biographies or commercial traction.

Investment becomes more credible when cohorts show repeated accepted handoffs, account expansion, paid conversion, and sustainable delivery costs. The principal risks are weak switching incentives, incumbent bundling, a workflow used only occasionally, and integration maintenance consuming the margin.

## Boundaries

No email, calendar, spreadsheets, slide editor, full project manager, or general-purpose AI assistant in the first release. Boardy can link to existing execution tools. A small next-action state is enough to close the loop without rebuilding them.
